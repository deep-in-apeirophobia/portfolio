# Bauhaus

The Bauhaus was a German art school, open from 1919 to 1933 in Weimar, Dessau and Berlin. It tried to join art, craft and industrial production. Its graphic design, shaped by László Moholy-Nagy, Herbert Bayer and Joost Schmidt, dropped ornament in favour of "elementary" means: primary colours, basic geometric shapes, sans-serif type and asymmetric layouts in which every element has a job. The idea is functional clarity that is also visually exciting, a poster that works like a machine.

## Key characteristics

- **Elementary geometry.** Compositions are built from circles, squares, triangles and heavy bars. Kandinsky's 1923 questionnaire tied yellow to the triangle, red to the square and blue to the circle.
- **Primary palette.** Red, yellow and blue with black and white, printed on off-white paper stock. Overprinting (inks overlapping) adds secondary tones.
- **Geometric sans-serif type, often lowercase.** Bayer's *universal* alphabet (1925) dropped capitals entirely. Futura (Renner, 1927) is the typeface of the period. Headlines are heavy and huge, labels small and tracked wide.
- **Dynamic asymmetry.** Layouts are not centred. Tension comes from off-axis balance, large empty areas, and text or bars set on diagonals or turned 90°.
- **Typophoto.** Moholy-Nagy's term for combining type with photography. Photos are usually black and white, high contrast, and cropped into geometric frames.
- **Rules and bars as structure.** Thick black lines divide, underline and point. They do real work, not decoration.
- **Flat, honest materials.** No gradients, shadows or bevels. Just ink on paper.

## How this redesign applies it

- **Hero as an exhibition poster.** The hero is laid out like a poster: a red circle, a yellow triangle, a blue square, a black ring and a vertical bar, plus a black band set at −28° carrying "vibrant — fast — scalable". Only the diagonal band scrolls (a slow marquee). The name "atrin hojjat" is set in heavy lowercase with a deliberately offset second line.
- **Bayer's lowercase rule.** All display text and microcopy are lowercase. The kicker "ausstellung", section labels ("werke", "kontakt", "werkzeug") and the "form folgt funktion" slogan printed on the blue square echo the school's German exhibition posters.
- **Kandinsky's shape–colour system as a UI system.** Each project cycles square/red, circle/blue, triangle/yellow. That pairing sets the shape behind the screenshot, the thick rule under the title, the index glyph and the arrow on "visit website". Stack chips carry small geometric bullets.
- **Typophoto images.** The portrait is set in a circle as a blue duotone, over a yellow square and a red corner triangle. Project screenshots are shown in black and white inside heavy black frames and turn to full colour on hover or focus. They stay recognisable while reading as period photomontage.
- **Jost** (a Futura revival) is the only typeface. Weight contrast (300 against 700–800) and tracked micro-labels create the hierarchy.
- **Structure from rules.** A 6px black rule under the masthead and between sections, a 14px bar above the about columns, the black "selected work" slab with a red base bar, and the blue contact field crossed by a black bar with a red sun.

## Learn more

- [Bauhaus — Tate art term](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/b/bauhaus): short overview from a major museum of the school's aims, teachers and influence.
- [Bauhaus Kooperation (bauhaus100)](https://www.bauhaus100.com/): the official network of the three Bauhaus collections, with deep articles on people, works and places.
- [Bauhaus-Archiv / Museum für Gestaltung, Berlin](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/): the world's largest Bauhaus collection, including posters, typography and Bayer's work.
- [Stiftung Bauhaus Dessau](https://bauhaus-dessau.de/en/): the foundation that runs the Dessau school building, where the typography and advertising workshop (Bayer, Schmidt) was based.
- [Bauhaus sources to download (bauhaus-bookshelf.org)](https://www.bauhaus-bookshelf.org/): scans of the original Bauhausbücher, including Moholy-Nagy's *Malerei, Fotografie, Film* (the "typophoto" essay).
- [Herbert Bayer (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Bayer): the designer behind the lowercase universal alphabet and many Bauhaus posters and prints.
- [Point and Line to Plane (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_and_Line_to_Plane): Kandinsky's 1926 Bauhaus book on the grammar of elementary forms.
