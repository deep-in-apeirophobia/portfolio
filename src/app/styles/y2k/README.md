# Y2K / Frutiger Aero

Frutiger Aero is the glossy, sky-blue look of consumer tech from roughly 2004 to 2013. It grew out of the late-90s Y2K
techno-optimism of the translucent iMac G3 and Mac OS X's "Aqua" interface (2000), and peaked with Windows Vista/7's
Aero glass, the Wii and early iPhone. The name (coined in 2017 by the Consumer Aesthetics Research Institute) nods to Adrian
Frutiger's humanist typeface and Microsoft's Aero. The philosophy was simple optimism: technology is clean, friendly,
natural and on your side, so interfaces were bright, wet-looking and full of nature imagery.

## Key characteristics
- **Gloss everywhere**: "gel" / "jelly" buttons with a bright highlight band on the top half, a hard mid-line and a
  glowing bottom, as if the control were a drop of coloured liquid under glass.
- **Glass and translucency**: Aero-style window frames and panels that blur and tint whatever is behind them, with
  diagonal reflection streaks and a thin white inner edge.
- **Nature meets tech**: blue skies, water droplets and bubbles, rolling green grass, aurora "swooshes", lens flares
  and sunbursts, often composited behind product UI.
- **Palette**: aqua and sky blue, lime/grass green and white, with small hits of orange and violet for icons; chrome and
  silver for logos and hardware.
- **Humanist, rounded type**: Frutiger, Segoe UI, Myriad and Lucida Grande for UI; bubbly rounded bold for headlines;
  chrome or gradient-filled word-marks with reflections.
- **Glossy object icons**: spherical orb icons and "Welcome Center" task lists with a big icon plus a title and
  one-line description.
- **Product-site layout**: centred content column, pill-shaped toolbar, glossy screenshots of the product shown on
  piano-black LCDs with floor reflections.

## How this redesign applies it
- The hero is a Vista-style desktop: a gradient sky with a lens flare, SVG aurora swooshes, floating CSS bubbles and
  rolling grass hills, all drawn with gradients and inline SVG (no images).
- "Atrin Hojjat" is set in Nunito Black as a chrome word-mark: a stacked metal gradient clipped to the text with a hard
  horizon line, a dark stroke and a drop shadow; the surname uses an aqua "liquid" version.
- A working **Welcome Center** window (Aero glass frame, caption buttons with the red close button, orb task icons)
  acts as the hero's secondary navigation to About, Projects, e-mail and GitHub.
- Every call to action is a gel pill button (blue, green or silver) with a highlight band and a shine sweep on hover;
  stack chips are small aqua gel capsules and the tech list is a row of glass bubbles.
- Project screenshots sit on glossy piano-black widescreen monitors with a blue power LED and chrome stand, next to
  frosted-glass cards numbered with coloured orbs, like a 2007 product tour ("Step 1 · Say hello", "Step 2 · Explore").
- Body copy uses Open Sans (a free stand-in for Segoe UI / Frutiger); bubbles stop animating under
  `prefers-reduced-motion`.

- [CARI: Frutiger Aero](https://cari.institute/aesthetics/frutiger-aero): the Consumer Aesthetics Research Institute's page for the aesthetic it named, with a large image gallery.
- [Frutiger Aero Archive](https://frutigeraeroarchive.org/): a curated archive of wallpapers, UI, ads and product imagery from the era.
- [Windows Aero (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_Aero): the design language of Vista and 7 (glass frames, Segoe UI, animations) and its guidelines.
- [Aqua (user interface) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqua_(user_interface)): Apple's gel-button, pinstripe and water-inspired interface that started the glossy era.
- [Frutiger (typeface) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frutiger_(typeface)): the humanist sans that gives the aesthetic its name and its typographic feel.
- [Glassmorphism: Definition and Best Practices (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/glassmorphism/): how translucent glass UI works today and how to keep it legible, useful for comparing it with the original Aero glass.
