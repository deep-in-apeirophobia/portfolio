# Russian Constructivism

Constructivism came out of the Soviet avant-garde after the 1917 Revolution. Artists like Alexander Rodchenko, Varvara Stepanova, El Lissitzky, Gustav Klutsis and the Stenberg brothers rejected "art for art's sake" and set out to *construct* useful things: posters, books, film advertising, textiles, kiosks. The artist was meant to be an engineer serving a new society. Graphic design became agitation (agitprop). Its tools were a strict red-and-black palette, the diagonal, the photograph and the shouted slogan. The movement peaked around 1919–1932, when Stalin's turn to Socialist Realism ended it.

## Key characteristics

- **Three inks only.** Revolutionary red, printer's black and the cream of cheap newsprint. Colour is a signal, not decoration.
- **The dynamic diagonal.** Compositions tilt roughly 15–45°, so the page feels like it's moving forward. Level, symmetrical layouts were seen as bourgeois and static.
- **Elementary geometry as symbol.** Wedges, circles, bars and arrows act out a message. The best-known example is Lissitzky's red wedge piercing the white circle in *Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge* (1919).
- **Heavy, hand-built sans capitals.** Condensed and blocky grotesks, often drawn by the designer, set in different sizes and angles. Type is treated as structure: it runs up the sides, along diagonals and in stacked blocks.
- **Photomontage.** High-contrast black-and-white photographs are cut into circles and wedges and combined with type and flat shapes (Klutsis, Rodchenko, Stepanova).
- **The shout.** Slogans with exclamation marks, megaphones and cones of text that grow in size, as in Rodchenko's 1924 Lengiz poster *Books!*, with Lilya Brik calling out through her cupped hand.
- **Numbered, machine-like order.** Numerals and "№" signs, rules and bars make the page look manufactured rather than handmade.

## How this redesign applies it

- **Hero as an agitprop poster.** A halftoned, grayscale portrait of Atrin shouts through a red megaphone-cone, a nod to Rodchenko's *Books!*. The hero message sits inside the cone and each word is set larger than the last. A black wedge pierces a red disc in the corner, echoing Lissitzky.
- **One tilt, everywhere.** The whole page shares a single `--tilt` angle (−10°) and nearby angles. The megaphone, the black and red bars, the name, the "О СЕБЕ / ABOUT" band, project labels and the tech band all lean. Body copy is kept level on the cream paper so it stays readable.
- **Strict palette and newsprint.** Only `#c8201a`, `#141210` and `#efe6d2` are used, with SVG noise grain for paper texture. All screenshots are forced to grayscale so they behave like photomontage material. Each one is cut into a parallelogram and overlaid with a multiplied red wedge.
- **Constructivist type.** The display face is Oswald (condensed poster capitals). The Cyrillic blocks, "РАБОТЫ", "ПИШИТЕ!" and the role bar, are set in Rubik Mono One (wide square-cut letters in the spirit of LEF). Body text is PT Sans from Moscow's ParaType. Russian words appear as graphic elements, each with an English gloss.
- **Numbered components.** The nav reads "№1 О СЕБЕ", "№2 РАБОТЫ", "№3 СВЯЗЬ". Projects carry giant red numerals, and names sit in black label bars. Stack chips look like stamped labels, and "Visit website" is a red arrow slab.
- **Motion kept short.** The words of the shout punch outward in sequence and the "About" band crawls slowly. All animations end visible and are switched off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

- [Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_the_Whites_with_the_Red_Wedge): background on the Lissitzky poster that defined the wedge-and-circle vocabulary.
- [Alexander Rodchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Rodchenko): his photography, photomontage and advertising posters, including *Books!* (1924).
- [Gustav Klutsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Klutsis): the leading pioneer of political photomontage.
- [Stenberg brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stenberg_brothers): film posters that turned Constructivist diagonals and montage into cinema graphics.
- [Rodchenko & Popova: Defining Constructivism, Tate Modern](https://www.tate.org.uk/whats-on/tate-modern/exhibition/rodchenko-popova): the exhibition page for Tate's landmark 2009 show on the movement's core figures.
