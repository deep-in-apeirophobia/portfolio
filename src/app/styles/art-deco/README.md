# Art Deco

Art Deco took its name from the 1925 Paris *Exposition internationale des Arts décoratifs et industriels modernes* and ran through the 1920s and 30s: skyscraper lobbies, ocean liners, cinemas, jewellery and travel posters. It mixed machine-age geometry with luxury materials such as lacquer, gold leaf, chrome and exotic veneers. Its philosophy was glamorous modernity: ornament was kept, but drawn with a ruler and compass, then set out in strict symmetry so the result felt both opulent and streamlined.

## Key characteristics

- **Geometric ornament**: sunbursts and fans, stepped ziggurat setbacks (like the Chrysler and Empire State crowns), chevrons, zigzags, lozenges and bundles of thin parallel lines.
- **Strict symmetry and a central axis**: compositions are centred and mirrored, like a building facade or a Cassandre poster.
- **Luxury palette**: black lacquer, deep emerald or navy, cream, and metallic gold or brass. Gold is shown as *foil*, a gradient with a bright highlight band, not a flat yellow.
- **Tall, geometric display type**: high-contrast capitals with stencil-like breaks (Broadway, Peignot, Bifur), set with generous letter-spacing. Thin monoline faces are used for secondary lines.
- **Framing**: double rules, stepped corner brackets, arched portals and cartouches turn every picture and panel into a plaque.
- **Stillness and polish**: any motion is slow and stately, like a sheen across metal or light from a marquee. Nothing bounces.

## How this redesign applies it

- **Type**: *Limelight* (a Deco marquee face with thick/thin contrast) for the name and headings, *Poiret One* (named after couturier Paul Poiret) for thin lines, *Josefin Sans* for widely tracked small caps, and *Marcellus* (a flared Roman serif) so body text stays readable.
- **Hero as a poster**: a centred axis runs from the nav through a fan ornament, the foil name, the role flanked by lozenges and a chevron divider, then the message. Behind it, a CSS `repeating-conic-gradient` sunburst rises from below the fold inside a double gold frame with stepped corners.
- **Foil, not yellow**: every gold surface uses a multi-stop gradient with a dark band (clipped to text, used as SVG gradients and as frame backgrounds), so it reads as metal. The name has a slow sheen that is turned off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Gilded plaques**: each project is a black lacquer panel with stepped corner ornaments, a Roman numeral ("No. III") and a screenshot set in a foil frame with a black fillet and bevel shadows. Stack chips are octagonal cartouches.
- **Architecture as ornament**: the About portrait sits in an arched doorway with a foil nameplate. The Works section uses emerald with pinstripes and chevron bands. The contact section is crowned with a stepped ziggurat, and each contact card has a small stepped crown.
- **Rhythm through symmetry**: the About paragraphs are mirrored columns around a double rule with a centre lozenge. The tech list is a 4×2 grid between rules, and the footer mirrors its two buttons around a centred credit.

## Learn more

- [An introduction to Art Deco (V&A)](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/an-introduction-to-art-deco): a museum overview of the style's origins, materials and global spread.
- [Art Deco (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco): a broad, well-sourced history with many examples of architecture, graphics and objects.
- [1925 Paris Exposition (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Exhibition_of_Modern_Decorative_and_Industrial_Arts): the exhibition that gave the style its name and set its vocabulary.
- [A. M. Cassandre (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassandre): the poster artist and type designer (Bifur, Peignot) whose centred, geometric compositions define Deco graphic design.
- [Chrysler Building (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Building): the classic source for sunburst crowns, stepped setbacks and metallic ornament.
- [Art Deco Society of New York](https://www.artdeco.org/): tours, archives and photography of surviving Deco interiors and facades.
- [Limelight on Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Limelight): the display face used here, a good starting point for Deco-inspired web typography.
