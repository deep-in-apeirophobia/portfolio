# Editorial

Editorial design is the visual language of print magazines and broadsheet newspapers. It grew from the modernist art direction of Alexey Brodovitch at *Harper's Bazaar* in the 1930s–50s and the grid-based newspaper redesigns that followed, and it lives on today in titles like *The New York Times Magazine*, *The Gentlewoman*, *Kinfolk* and *Monocle*. It serves the reader: typography carries the hierarchy, whitespace is used on purpose, and each spread is paced like an article, not like a list of cards.

## Key characteristics

- **Serif-led typography.** A high-contrast display serif (Didone or transitional) for headlines, a calm book serif for running text, and a small, tracked-out sans for kickers, bylines, captions and folios.
- **An article's parts.** Every story has a kicker (section label), a headline, a standfirst (dek), a byline, body copy, captions, pull quotes and an end mark (■) that shows where the story finishes.
- **Multi-column grids.** Body copy is set in narrow columns with column rules, justified or ragged-right, with hyphenation. Images span several columns, and layouts alternate from spread to spread.
- **Hairline and double rules.** Thin rules divide sections. The thick-and-thin double rule under the masthead is a classic newspaper motif.
- **Paper and ink.** A warm off-white stock, near-black ink and a single accent colour, used sparingly for kickers, drop caps and pull quotes.
- **Print furniture.** A masthead with nameplate, volume, issue and date lines, a contents box, running folios with page numbers, and a colophon.
- **Restrained motion.** Print does not move. On screen, the most you'd see is a quiet fade-in and plain colour changes on hover.

## How this redesign applies it

- **Masthead as hero.** A dateline (Vol. I — No. 1 · Autumn 2026 · The Portfolio Issue) sits above a Playfair Display Black nameplate. Under it is a thick-and-thin double rule and a section nav written as "p.04 The Profile", like a magazine's page index.
- **Cover story plus contents.** The hero message is set as a cover headline, with the italic words in the accent red. The tagline is the standfirst, and there is a byline. A ruled "In This Issue" column on the right lists the sections, and the Logo Diffusion screenshot appears there as "On the cover".
- **About as an interview.** The two About paragraphs become the answers in a Q&A profile, set in two justified columns with a column rule. It adds a drop-cap introduction, a pull quote framed by thick and thin rules, and a grayscale portrait with a caption.
- **Projects as features.** The lead feature (Logo Diffusion) gets a huge headline, a full-width figure with a "Fig. 1" caption and credit line, body copy in two columns, and a "By the numbers" sidebar. The other four alternate between image-left and image-right. Each has a department kicker (Health, Civic Technology…), a standfirst taken from the project's first sentence, a drop cap and an end mark.
- **Stack as "Filed under".** Tech chips become a small-caps index line separated by middots, and "Visit website" is set as an italic display link with the hostname in small caps.
- **Correspondence and colophon.** The contact section is set as "Letters to the Developer". The tech list is typeset inside a double-ruled colophon box, and folios such as "21 · The Portfolio Issue · Autumn 2026" run between sections.

## Learn more

- [Butterick's Practical Typography](https://practicaltypography.com/): a free, opinionated guide to body text, line length, rules and hierarchy, the basics of every good editorial page.
- [Practical Typography: Hyphenation](https://practicaltypography.com/hyphenation.html): why justified columns need hyphenation, and when to set text ragged instead.
- [The Elements of Typographic Style (Robert Bringhurst)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elements_of_Typographic_Style): the standard reference on page proportion, measure and serif typography.
- [Alexey Brodovitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Brodovitch): the *Harper's Bazaar* art director who invented the modern magazine spread.
- [Society of Publication Designers](https://www.spd.org/): the editorial design industry's awards body. Its annual winners are the best current magazine layouts.
- [Kinfolk](https://www.kinfolk.com/): contemporary slow-living editorial design, with generous margins, quiet serifs and captioned photography.
- [Initial (drop cap)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial): the history and variants of the drop cap, from illuminated manuscripts to magazine openers.
