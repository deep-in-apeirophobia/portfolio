# Swiss / International Typographic Style

The International Typographic Style grew out of the Zurich and Basel design schools in the 1950s and 60s, led by Josef Müller-Brockmann, Armin Hofmann and Emil Ruder, and was carried abroad by designers such as Massimo Vignelli. Its designers wanted graphic design to be objective and universal. Content is arranged on a mathematical grid and set in a neutral sans-serif, and hierarchy comes from size, weight and position, not ornament. The designer's aim is to communicate clearly, without self-expression.

## Key characteristics

- **Modular grid.** Every element sits on a column grid (often 12 columns, sometimes a square module), with consistent gutters and a steady vertical rhythm. The grid is the structure of the page, and designers often leave it visible.
- **Neo-grotesque sans-serif.** The standard faces are Akzidenz-Grotesk, Helvetica and Univers. Display sizes use tight tracking and tight leading. Text is set in only one or two weights.
- **Flush-left, ragged-right text.** Text is never justified or centred. Word spacing stays even, and the ragged edge is left alone.
- **Extreme scale contrast.** Very large headlines sit next to small captions and labels. The distance between the sizes creates the hierarchy.
- **Asymmetric composition and whitespace.** Blocks are placed off-centre and generous empty space is left on purpose. The white space is part of the design, not a gap to fill.
- **Restrained palette and objective imagery.** The page uses black, white and one signal colour (usually red). Photographs are documentary and often black and white, with no illustration or decoration.
- **Numbered, labelled information.** Sections are numbered, and label/value pairs, captions ("Fig. 1") and rules organise content the way a technical document would.

## How this redesign applies it

- The hero works like a Müller-Brockmann concert poster. "Build / vibrant, fast / and scalable / web apps / with me." is set in bold Inter Tight at about 10vw with -0.055em tracking. Each line starts on a different grid column (1, 3, 1, 5, 5). Only the last line uses the signal red.
- The grid is visible. Hairline red column guides for the 12-column grid are drawn over the page (4 columns on phones). The masthead has a "Grid on/off" toggle, and the `G` key also toggles it, so you can see how every block snaps to the columns.
- There is no decoration. Hierarchy comes only from size, weight and position: large red section numbers (1, 2, 3), heavy black rules, grey labels over bold values ("Name / Atrin Hojjat"), and small "Fig. 2.3" captions under images.
- All photography is treated in black and white (`grayscale` plus extra contrast). Project screenshots return to colour on hover, which keeps them recognisable without breaking the palette.
- The layout is asymmetric. Project rows alternate the image between columns 6–12 and 1–7, with the text set in a narrow flush-left column beside it. Stack chips are plain rectangles with a 1px black rule.
- Contact is set as a solid black field with oversized email addresses. It ends with a cropped "Atrin." wordmark in the manner of Swiss poster typography.

## Learn more

- [International Typographic Style (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Typographic_Style): a concise overview of the movement's origins, principles and key figures.
- [Josef Müller-Brockmann (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_M%C3%BCller-Brockmann): the author of *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* and of the Tonhalle concert posters that inspired this hero.
- [Emil Ruder (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Ruder): the Basel teacher whose book *Typographie* codified the style's approach to rhythm, contrast and white space.
- [Armin Hofmann (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armin_Hofmann): Ruder's Basel colleague, known for reduced, high-contrast posters and a form-first teaching method.
- [The Vignelli Canon (PDF, RIT Vignelli Center)](https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf): Massimo Vignelli's free short book on grids, type scale and discipline.
- [eMuseum, Museum für Gestaltung Zürich](https://www.emuseum.ch/): the museum's collection of original Swiss posters, searchable by designer.
- [The Grid System](https://www.thegridsystem.org/): a curated archive of articles, books and tools about grid-based design.
